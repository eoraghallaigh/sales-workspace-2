### **Cross-rep synthesis**

#### What held up

* One place to work the play. This was universally liked.

* Agent-written, research-grounded, editable sequences. All three liked this once they understood it. Paul conditionally, Leanne and Oisín enthusiastically. This is the strongest part of the concept.

* Plays give focus. Reps prefer to work batch companies together with a theme so they can work fluently, rather than context-switch between different prospecting pitches. 

* Working a play company the same way as a non-play company is important. In a rep’s mind a play is just a theme, so the execution should be the same either way. 

#### Where it breaks

* The UI emphasises pre-generated sequences, which landed well, but **reps also need granular control over tasks to remind them to follow-up**. While the sequencing agent will create call tasks, it’s probably worth giving the rep a quick way to insert their own tasks into a sequence without having to prompt the agent every time.

* Generic/net-new sequences are wrong for already-engaged accounts. The agent must produce continuation-aware sequences for accounts with history, and the rep has to be able to see that it did.

* Reps don’t fully trust the contact list from Marketing, because they frequently find errors in portal 53 data. Marketing’s contacts should be strong strong recommendations, but the rep should be able to add to them.

* No prioritisation / reason-to-work on the play list. All three asked some form of “where do I start / why this company?” The MVP shows a list without ranking or a visible reason. Needs a clear sort criteria, or “why it’s in the play” explanation.

* Missing Hub/portal summary in the work view. For install-base prospecting it’s the first thing reps check; without it they leave the workspace, breaking the one-place promise and company-work attribution.
