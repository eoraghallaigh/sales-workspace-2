# Prototype Test — Research Findings

8 reps tested the prototype: Sarah Li, Kira Hoff, Owen Beecham, Callum Breen, George Archer, Sophie Ball, Anya Katre, Theo Hector. Marc Fernandez Veiga and Conor McDonald observed some sessions.

The sequences in the prototype weren't actually generated by AI, the content was hand-authored placeholder. So the trust findings (RQ1–RQ4) lean on reps' lived experience with HubSpot's existing automated sequences and their own Glean/ChatGPT/Gemini workflows.

---

## What is reps' current experience with automated outreach?

Mixed, but cautiously positive. Reps say the writing has gotten better and rarely does harm. The bigger problem is targeting, not content. Autos fire at unqualified leads, dormant accounts, or customers reps have already handled. A secondary complaint is that the writing still reads as AI (em dashes, "not X, it's Y", too long, too generic). Several reps are shifting away from canned sequences toward one-to-one outreach drafted with Glean, ChatGPT, or Gemini.

> "If I were to put on a scale of one to five, I would say it's a three or a four. It's definitely not a one or a two… I think it's when they go out to not qualified people. That is more the issue." — Kira Hoff [00:10:11]

> "The messaging is definitely improved… the language is a lot more conversational… [but] certain QLs that for us would be extremely high intent where an automated message almost goes out instantly… that's the only, I guess, red flag." — Anya Katre [00:12:02]

> "We're really making the shift away from sequences and more personalized one on one messaging. So very personalized, targeted, very relevant." — Sarah Li [00:18:18]

> "I don't think I've gotten as many conversions, meeting conversions and things based on that standalone email… It's more of a prompt for me." — Sophie Ball [00:15:37]

Theo was the outlier here. He's uniformly positive on the existing autos, and gets meetings booked directly off them.

Opportunity:
Fix the targeting before tuning the writing. Reps don't trust the *who* nearly as much as they trust the *what*.

---

## What would have to be true for a rep to trust an AI-drafted call script, LinkedIn DM, or email enough to send it with light edits?

Two things: it can't read as AI, and the org/contact data it uses has to be right.

Reps named the same AI tells over and over. Em dash. "Not X, it's Y" sentences. Too long. Generic openers. Salesy "leaving ROI on the table" lines. And data that's been substituted poorly ("HubSpot Inc. Corp." instead of "HubSpot"). When those are absent, and the message focuses on the prospect's problem rather than pitching HubSpot, reps said they'd send with light edits.

> "One is the EM dash. Yeah, that one's obvious. Two is brevity. It's sometimes a little too lengthy. And then the third one… is the it's not X, it's Y sentence structure." — Kira Hoff [00:25:01]

> "This dash here, the double dash is the biggest one for me. I know it's literarily correct but humans just don't use it. And the next one is just the general sentence structure. It's not this, it's this." — George Archer [00:30:00]

> "It is dependent on the org data, for example… instead of us being called HubSpot, we're called HubSpot Inc. Corp. It shows up in the sequences as HubSpot Inc. Corp." — Owen Beecham [00:14:36]

> "As a prospect, I just want you to focus on my problem. I don't want you to be trying to say, oh, HubSpot can solve this." — Callum Breen [00:20:47]

> "If we can okay it before it gets sent out too, that would save so much time and it would prevent these sequences being sent out the wrong people… it takes 20 seconds. That's so much faster than having to write the email myself." — Kira Hoff [00:26:55]

Theo and Callum both said the prototype's hand-authored content already looked too AI-generated and would need editing. Useful signal: whatever the real agent ships, reps will scrutinise it the same way.

Opportunities:
- Prompt-engineer the agent to suppress em dashes, "not X, it's Y" structure, and overly long emails.
- Let reps set per-org or per-rep tone preferences (Owen wanted a profile that learned more calls vs. more emails, formal vs. casual).

## How do reps actually review and verify outreach content today?

When reps use their own AI workflow (Glean, ChatGPT, Gemini, Lovable), they almost always read and rewrite. They check the company URL and LinkedIn, double-check that the contact's job title is actually current, strip AI tells, shorten, remove "selling HubSpot" copy, and verify the hook is real and recent. Job titles came up a lot because HubSpot data often has stale roles.

> "Sometimes there can be a bit of a glitch or something in terms of job title and just double verifying that before speaking to the contact." — Sophie Ball [00:04:02]

> "I look at their website, I looked at their LinkedIn, I looked at. Now I also use Gemini for research." — Sarah Li [00:17:50]

> "It's not a three. So I have the sequence… the first task in the sequence is just a task. It's not an automated email. It's a manual email. It's insert personalized email here. So I'm starting the task, going to Glean. I want you to send me, write me a first email. Keep it short." — Kira Hoff [00:21:39]

> "Where is it picking this information up from and how accurate it is and how recent it is. Because obviously if this is from end of last year, then it's maybe not as recent." — Anya Katre [00:27:35]

> "I would be looking at what language is it speaking in… if it's too formal, if it sounds AI… Is there too many words in here?… are we looking to really delve into this friction point that they're feeling." — Callum Breen [00:18:35]

Theo and Owen do less personalisation at the content level. They take a sequence template and tweak it. Their review is lighter. The reps doing the most rigorous review (Sophie, Kira, Callum, Anya) are the same reps already running their own AI loops.

## Does the prototype's design support that review-and-verify workflow?

Partly. The pre-generated sequence with inline content was a clear win as a time-saver. The strategy page surfaces the right ingredients (company data, intent, key stakeholders, prior deals, recent activity) without pushing the rep out into other tabs.

The gaps:

- "View Reasoning" at the bottom of the sequence is hard to find. Most reps missed it or thought it wasn't for them.
- Provenance and freshness of cited signals isn't visible enough. When the strategy says "impressive growth metrics", reps want to know which metrics and when.
- There's no obvious way to regenerate or to teach the agent ("always say HubSpot, not HubSpot Inc. Corp.").

> "Straight away, I can see that this is giving me everything that I need without having to then click View Record, and then pull that record into the separate tab. It's all keeping it within the workspace." — Callum Breen [00:14:05]

> "If I look at any contacts in what we're doing at the moment, to do some general call research and the sequences, you're looking at 10, 15 to 20 minutes per person… Whereas now, you don't need to do that. You can just come in, call them straight away." — George Archer [00:18:14]

> "I did see it [View Reasoning]. It didn't stand out to me. I think potentially maybe it should have been at the top because I'm having to first read the emails and then it's here." — Anya Katre [00:28:08]

> "When they say impressive growth metrics, that could mean anything. So what is that?" — Anya Katre [00:27:44]

George was happy not to edit the agent's prompt at all ("you guys are the experts in the products"). So the prompt-editing affordance is welcome but doesn't need to be prominent. The signal provenance does.

Opportunities:
- Pull View Reasoning up the page, or surface the strongest reasoning inline next to each signal.
- Show source and date on every signal cited on the strategy page.

## When a rep has a connected call with a contact, is automatically pausing or unenrolling the email sequence the correct default?

Not a clean yes. Reps want pause by default with easy unpause, not hard unenrollment. The strongest counter-argument: a "connected call" can also mean "they said call back later" and then ghosted. In that case the rep needs the sequence to keep running.

Anya and Kira leaned toward "yes, pause". Sarah explicitly asked for a manual pause option. George said keep it rolling unless there's a real reply or booking.

> "That depends how the call is going. Yeah, sometimes they hang up and then I want the sequence to run. Maybe if there's an option to pause it." — Sarah Li [00:24:10]

> "Outside of a reply or a connect call, I would say no, it's more beneficial to keep it rolling… Again, it's a tricky one. So it depends on what the connect call is. It might just be, well, I can't speak at the moment. Give me a call back later. And then they just don't answer anyway." — George Archer [00:27:46]

> "Say they are in a sequence and my call task is set to them for tomorrow, but instead I call them today and I get in touch and we have a chat. Then would I have to go manually unenroll them?… Yeah, I think that seems a logical thing." — Anya Katre [00:31:29]

> "Because there's such a high volume of people that are going to be in sequences managing each one individually that, pausing and unpausing… I know personally for me I just wouldn't get around to doing it. Yeah, just letting the agents take care of it." — George Archer [00:28:58]

The call outcome matters. A real conversation should pause. A "couldn't talk, call back" shouldn't. The product probably needs either rep-confirmed call disposition, or pause by default with a single-click un-pause right where the sequence is shown.

Opportunity:
Pause on connected call by default, but make un-pause one click from the strategy page so a rep can flip it back if the call was just a "call me later".

## Beyond connected calls, what other moments or events should automatically end a sequence?

Just reply and meeting booked. Nothing else came up unprompted.

> "If they booked a meeting?… Usually if they respond. Yeah, I would say that's the biggest thing." — Sarah Li [00:23:38]

> "Outside of a reply or a connect call, I would say no." — George Archer [00:27:46]

> "If they reply. If they, I guess, pick up your phone, hopefully that should already be in the system." — Anya Katre [00:32:51]

Sarah raised a related but separate request worth flagging here. When a sequence is paused or unenrolled, the unsent content disappears. She wants it preserved so she can copy it into a one-off follow-up.

> "Right now when you pause or unenroll someone from a sequence, the whole sequence goes away. So if I had a personalized email that was supposed to go out in three days that I spent 10 minutes on, I can't see what I wrote." — Sarah Li [00:24:50]

Opportunity:
When a sequence pauses or unenrolls, keep the queued content visible on the contact so the rep can reuse it.

## Can reps find and use the affordance for editing AI-drafted outreach without prompting?

Mostly no. 6 of 8 reps only found the edit affordance after I prompted them. The text doesn't read as editable — no border, no edit icon, no strong hover state. Sophie called it "a me problem" but every rep had the same problem. That's a design issue, not a user issue.

> "Because it wasn't in box. I had no idea I could edit it… As silly as that sounds." — Owen Beecham [00:18:39]

> "Only because… it came up highlighted and I clicked on it and saw that I could edit." — George Archer [00:21:13]

> "Oh, clicked edit. That's how I would do it… No, that's it. That's definitely a me problem. That was very obvious." — Sophie Ball [00:27:23] *(only after prompting)*

> "Yeah, I don't know. As in, if I was putting the sequence, it was a normal sequence, I presume I would just edited away, but I don't know. Would I be just training the AI to, to get rid of that? I'm not sure." — Sophie Ball [00:26:53]

Callum and Sarah found it quickly. They were the minority.

Owen's concrete suggestion: "if you even put a box… thing around the outside." Owen also wanted bulk find/replace ("place all company name dynamic fields"). Marc (observing) suggested an explicit edit-mode toggle with save/discard.

Sophie's question is also a real one and a separate problem from discoverability. Reps don't know whether their edit just changes this email, or also trains the agent.

Opportunities:
- Put a visible edit mode on the sequence content. Border, hover state, or an explicit Edit button.
- Tell the rep what their edit does. Just this email? This sequence? The agent's behaviour next time?

---

## Once edits are discovered, do reps feel safe making them?

Yes. Once a rep realises they can edit, no one hesitates. Editing is a familiar pattern from existing sequences and from their own Glean/ChatGPT workflows. The hesitation is upstream (discoverability) and downstream (does my edit train the model?), not in the act of editing itself.

> "Oh, I do like how I can just edit it right there… I like that. That doesn't require another step." — Sarah Li [00:16:08]

> "Yeah, just on that. Yeah, easy to find out. So I'll just come in, just change it, whatever needs to be done. Look at the next one. Yeah, change that next one. Change that. Yeah, good to go. But again, I wouldn't spend too much time on it." — George Archer [00:21:24]

> "It's editable, right?… So I could go in. Oh, fantastic. So I could go in." — Callum Breen [00:19:55]

This is the cleanest good-news finding in the set. Editing isn't scary, it's just hidden.

## Do reps want to execute one-off calls, build lists for calling later, or some combination of both?

Both. The mix is personal. Every rep does some one-off calling straight from the workspace or contact card, and every rep also uses task queues or self-built lists for batched calling (power hours, blitzes).

The default workflow most reps described: try a one-off call first because it's low cost and high upside. If no answer, enroll them in a sequence that creates a follow-up call task.

> "I only build lists if it's a blitz. Otherwise I am manually going, one by one… I usually like to call before I put them in a sequence because I don't want to spend 10, 15 minutes crafting a personalized sequence. If I can get them on the phone on the first call, and then I get a quick yes or no." — Sarah Li [00:21:41]

> "I'll sometimes just call from workspace. Yeah, I'll do that. But if it's a first call, I usually want to jump in, take a quick look, just double check area code." — Kira Hoff [00:27:46]

> "I do a mix. I do have my own task queue called daily call. So this is something I would set up the day before… and then I have an hour every day that's blocked out… And then as I go through the day, any, for example, if a QL comes in or something stands out, then I'll just give them a call on a more ad hoc level." — Anya Katre [00:29:11]

> "Really and truly, it's company [by company]. But I know we're supposed to do tasks. I've got so many tasks, I get inundated with tasks. Given that we've got sequences going out left, right and center. So I just call them." — Theo Hector [00:06:26]

> "I try to keep the call tasks focused to people who are engaging and then the net new people usually will be in dynamic sequences where they'll only create call tasks if people are opening or clicking." — George Archer [00:11:24]

Theo and George both flagged that call-task volume from sequences gets so big it gets ignored.

> "There's 400 call tasks… I'll never get through all these so I just won't even bother." — George Archer [00:11:00]

Building lists for calling later only works if the list is the right size. The prototype's Daily Power Hour (~30 contacts) landed well with Anya and Theo for exactly that reason — it bounds the queue.

Opportunity:
Keep the Daily Power Hour bounded and short. The moment it grows past what a rep can finish in their blocked hour, it stops getting opened.
